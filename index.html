How it works

Getting your own data in. In Meltwater, open the Ember search, choose Export, and save the CSV. Drop that file onto the box at the top of this page (several files at once is fine; duplicate rows are removed by Document ID). Everything runs in your browser; nothing is uploaded anywhere. Reload the page to return to the sample week.

What happens to each row. Rows matching a known false-positive pattern are excluded. Every other row goes through a relevance check: the text around each "Ember" is scanned for energy-context terms (think tank, electricity, coal, solar, grid, data centres, Ember's spokespeople, in the languages Ember is covered in) and for other-Ember terms (TVs, cookware, games, crypto, AI models, venues). Clear cases are decided automatically; unclear ones go to "Needs review" and stay out of the numbers until someone presses Yes or No on the Articles tab. Those decisions are remembered in your browser and travel with the rules JSON. Rows with no text (licensing walls such as "This content is licensed by the CLA") are counted as blocked. Every remaining row is matched against the report registry, top to bottom; the first rule whose patterns match wins, and unmatched rows go to "Other Ember mention". Copies of the same headline are grouped into one story, with the best-tier, earliest copy marked as primary. The mention type (headline, quoted, report cited, data cited, passing mention) is the strongest found across all copies of a story.

When a new report launches. Go to Tagging rules, add a row near the top with the report name, its full title, topic, the region the report is about, and a few distinctive phrases from the report (its title in each language it was covered in, a headline number, the spokesperson's name), then press Re-tag. Check the Articles tab filtered to that report. Copy the rules JSON and share it with the team so everyone tags the same way.

Reading the numbers. Hits are export rows; stories are distinct headlines. Reach is Meltwater's audience estimate and only useful for relative comparison; AVE is deliberately ignored. Coverage region is where the outlet is based; report region is what the Ember work is about. Compare regions on quoted share and tier-1 share, not raw counts, because the English-language and aggregator universe is much larger.

Limits. Tagging is keyword-based, so it needs a quick check for each new export, especially the "Other" bucket. Bylines are whatever Meltwater captured. Blocked rows can only be fixed at the Meltwater end (full-text licensing for CLA sources).
